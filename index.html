What the hybrid keeps from Pfitz

  • Every Sunday: 15K race, long runs to 34 km, MP runs of 13/16/19/23 km
  • Three tune-ups (as time trials) and the race-week dress rehearsal
  • Recovery weeks at 12 and 8 weeks to goal, and the 3-week taper

What it changes

  • Tue/Thu/Sat sub-threshold reps instead of LT tempos and VO2max 600 m/1 km reps
  • Saturday goes easy before MP Sundays, long runs of 31+ km and time trials
  • 6 run days every week, Monday off, Wednesday recovery, Friday easy
  • Reps lengthen toward race prep (4 x 3 km)

How to run each session

Easy and recovery runs

  • Conversational the whole way; recovery runs slower than easy days
  • Go by feel or heart rate, not pace, especially in wind or heat

Strides

  • 6-10 x 100 m (about 15-20 s), building to roughly 5K pace, relaxed and tall
  • Near the end of the run once warm, walk or jog back to recover
  • Jog 3-5 min easy afterwards, or do them on the way home

Hill sprints

  • 6 x 10 s hard uphill on a steep-ish slope, about 95% effort
  • In the last third of the run, before strides and the cool-down
  • Walk back down for full recovery (1-2 min); stop if form breaks down

Sub-threshold reps

  • 2-3 km warm-up and cool-down; short jog between reps
  • Comfortably hard, controlled breathing; finish feeling you could do 1-2 more
  • Pace off lap averages, not instant pace; ease off into headwinds rather than chase pace

LT tempo (Pfitz)

  • Warm up 3-4 km, then a continuous run at 15K to half-marathon pace
  • Even pace; the last kilometres should feel hard but sustainable

VO2max reps (Pfitz)

  • Warm up 3-4 km with a few strides; reps at about 5K pace
  • Jog 50-90% of the rep time between reps; aim for even splits

Marathon-pace runs

  • Easy start, then the MP section in the second half of the run
  • Practise race fuel, drinks, kit and shoes
  • Hold goal pace, don't beat it

Long runs

  • Start easy and settle; finishing slightly faster is fine
  • On runs over 90 min, take a gel every 5 km to practise race fuelling

Time trials and tune-ups

  • Flat, measured route; 3 km warm-up with strides, 2-3 km cool-down
  • Start slightly conservative, race the second half
  • Use the result to check marathon pace and update paces above

Nutrition

Based on 85 kg body weight and 190 cm height. Numbers follow mainstream sports-nutrition guidance (grams of carbs per kg of body weight). Adjust them to how you feel and to your gut. If your stomach keeps causing problems, see a sports dietitian.

Gel: SiS Beta Fuel gel, 40 g carbs (maltodextrin and fructose). It isn't isotonic, so take it with about 150-250 ml of water.

Before long runs
  • Evening before:
    • Normal runs: a normal carb-rich dinner, about 130-170 g of carbs.
    • 31+ km long runs, MP Sundays and time trials: eat 7-8 g/kg the day before (600-680 g) as a mini carb load. Use the long-run day above, plus a bigger dinner.
  • Early-morning start (the default): within an hour of running, eat 40-60 g of something easy to digest. For example, toast with jam, or a Beta Fuel gel with 300 ml of water.
  • During runs over 90 minutes: a Beta Fuel gel every 5 km, with water.
    • That's about 80 g an hour at long-run pace and about 90 g an hour at marathon pace.
    • That rate is at the top of what the gut can absorb, so it needs practice.
Race-time rehearsals

On two runs, copy race day exactly, starting at race time (10:00; confirm the start time in the race guide).

For each rehearsal:

  • Day before: eat the long-run day plus a bigger dinner, about 600-680 g of carbs.
  • Race-day routine: breakfast at 06:30-07:00 as in the race-week table below, caffeine at about 09:00 if you'll use it on race day, and a gel at 09:45.
  • During the run: gels every 5 km, in race kit and shoes.
Gut training
  • On the other MP Sundays and the 31-34 km long runs, practise the gels at the same spacing even on early starts.
  • Note how your stomach reacts. If it complains at every 5 km, try every 6-7 km in training and build back up.
Hydration and sodium
  • Sweat test: on two or three long runs, weigh yourself without clothes before and after. Each 1 kg lost is about 1 L of sweat.
  • How much to drink: keep losses under 2-3% of body weight (1.7-2.5 kg). Usually that's 400-800 ml an hour, more in heat.
  • Sodium: add electrolytes on long or warm runs, and on race morning, especially if you're a salty sweater (white marks on kit).
